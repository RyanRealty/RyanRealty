# Brand voice anchors — research findings (2026-08-05)

Matt's directive: find an existing, documented voice to adopt across the site and the
reports. The current report copy overeditorializes: it explains what numbers mean,
moralizes ("...which is one more reason the list price has to be right on day one"),
and coins aphorisms ("Pricing sets the number. Competition decides how it lands.").
The fix is an external anchor, not another round of my defaults.

Ten voices researched across four tracks. Every URL below was fetched and verified
live on 2026-08-05.

---

## The shortlist, ranked for our use

### 1. Redfin data journalism — the structure

The closest working analog to what our reports should be. Their market reports are
read by millions of homeowners.

- Read: https://www.redfin.com/news/ (verified example: the 2026-08-05 affordability report)
- Headline is a complete claim with a number, never a topic label.
- A bulleted key-facts block precedes all prose. The report is readable in 15 seconds.
- Methodology is stated in plain terms before any interpretation.
- **Interpretation lives only inside a named human's quote.** The writer's own voice
  never says what a number means. For us: any judgment goes in a quote from the
  signing broker, under their name.
- Numbers carry the verbs: "fell 2.2%," never "there was a decline of."
- A data table gives receipts under every claim.

### 2. GOV.UK style guide — the mechanical law

The world's most rigorous plain-language standard. Open Government Licence: we can
legally adapt it into our own guide, verbatim where useful.

- Read: https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/
- Its verbatim position: "adjectives can be subjective and make the text sound more
  emotive and like spin." Adjectives-as-persuasion are banned outright.
- Sentences 15 to 20 words. Front-load the fact. Address the reader as "you."
- Tone: "brisk, but not terse; serious but not pompous."

### 3. Buffett's shareholder letters — the register

Fifty years of financial writing for one imagined non-expert reader.

- Read: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html
- Codified: Lawrence Cunningham, *The Essays of Warren Buffett*; Buffett's foreword
  to the SEC's Plain English Handbook (https://www.sec.gov/pdf/handbook.pdf).
- Write to ONE homeowner, not "sellers." Plain word over formal word ("about," not
  "approximately"). Name the limitation before the strength. State a number once and
  let it carry the point. Never stack adjectives on a number.
- Filter out: his homespun one-liners. Borrow the discipline, not the folksiness.

### 4. The Economist Style Guide — the self-check

- Read free (their own CDN): https://cdn.static-economist.com/sites/default/files/pdfs/style_guide_12.pdf
- The rule that names our exact failure: "Do not be too pleased with yourself."
  Also: do not be hectoring, do not be too chatty, cut anything cuttable.

### Useful but partial

- **Stripe** (https://slab.com/blog/stripe-writing-culture/): define a term once, show
  the mechanism, no adjectives standing in for facts. Take the precision, not the
  developer-doc coldness.
- **37signals** (https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/09.7-copywriting-is-interface-design):
  "Copywriting is interface design." Real words, no filler, every label scrutinized.
  Good for site UI copy.
- **Axios Smart Brevity** (https://www.axioshq.com/hubfs/smart-brevity-101.pdf): the
  one-fact lede + bolded number works for a report's summary block. Wall-to-wall it
  reads gimmicky (CJR: "a steamrolling of nuance").
- **Mailchimp** (https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/): well-built, but its licensed
  playfulness is wrong for a valuation document, and its CC-BY-NC license restricts
  verbatim reuse.
- **Atlassian** (https://atlassian.design/content/voice-and-tone-principles/): take
  only the tone-by-situation dial (a buyer's-market verdict is written like an error
  state: no cleverness). Discard the personality.
- **Howard Marks / Oaktree** (https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos): one
  transferable habit: hedge words only on genuine estimates, never on known numbers.
- **Vanguard Plain Talk**: retired in 2019; an archived SEC filing artifact, not a
  living corpus. Not recommended.

## The same passages, before and after

### Passage 1 — seasonality

**Now shipping:**
> 450 sales say the calendar matters. ... The bar is the median number of days those
> homes took to go pending. Lower is faster. ... This is history, not a forecast: it
> tells you when Bend buyers have been most active, and a home priced on the evidence
> in this report does not need to wait for a hot month to sell.

**GOV.UK register:**
> When Bend homes go pending. 7,205 single-family sales closed in Bend over the last
> 3 years, grouped by close month. April sales went pending in a median of 9 days.
> December sales took 39.

**Redfin register:**
> April homes went pending in 9 days, December homes in 39. Based on 7,205
> single-family sales closed in Bend, August 2023 to August 2026, grouped by close
> month. Months with fewer than 12 sales are excluded.

**Buffett register:**
> We pulled every Bend sale from the last three years, 7,205 of them. April homes
> found their buyer in 9 days at the median. December homes took 39. We can't tell
> you next April will repeat that. We can tell you it has held for three years.

### Passage 2 — the price band

**Now shipping:**
> Between $504,000 and $616,000 in Bend right now, a buyer sees 131 active listings
> ... Those are the homes yours is judged against on day one. ... Pricing sets the
> number. Competition decides how it lands.

**GOV.UK register:**
> 131 homes are for sale in Bend between $504,000 and $616,000. Their median asking
> price is $570,000, and they have been on the market a median of 53 days. 68 more
> are pending in the same range.

**Buffett register:**
> List at $609,000 and you join 131 sellers asking between $504,000 and $616,000.
> The median one has waited 53 days. 68 have found their buyer and are pending.

### Passage 3 — the failed listing

**Now shipping:**
> The market gave its answer. Here is what it was worth hearing. ... That history is
> built into the numbers in this report. The gap between the comparable math and the
> recommendation is the part we fix together: condition, presentation, and timing.

**GOV.UK register:**
> Your last listing asked $619,999 and did not sell. Over the last 3 years, 3,394
> Central Oregon homes failed to sell and later sold. The median one closed at 94.2%
> of its failed asking price. 12.3% sold above it. The recommendation in this report
> reflects those outcomes.

**Redfin register (interpretation moved into the broker's named quote):**
> Homes that fail to sell close at a median 94.2% of the ask that failed. 3,394
> failed-then-sold Central Oregon homes, 2023 to 2026. 12.3% beat their old ask.
>
> "The second listing succeeds by correcting the first ask, not by defending it,"
> said Matt Ryan, principal broker.

## Recommendation

Adopt a three-layer anchor, each layer from a documented source:

1. **Structure: Redfin's report conventions.** Complete-claim headlines, key-facts
   block first, methodology before interpretation, data-table receipts, and the big
   one: interpretation appears only inside the signing broker's named quote.
2. **Law: GOV.UK.** Adjectives are spin. 15 to 20 word sentences. Front-load the
   fact. Legally adaptable into voice_guidelines.md under the Open Government
   Licence.
3. **Register: Buffett.** One reader. Plain words. Name limits honestly. A number
   stated once, then left alone. The Economist's "do not be too pleased with
   yourself" as the review test.

What dies under this anchor: aphorism pairs, meaning-narration ("this tells you..."),
sermon clauses ("...which is one more reason..."), drama headers, and every sentence
that explains the sentence before it.
