# The Studio Pipeline — how top motion studios move from brief to screen

Research date: 2026-07-09. The stage discipline of Buck, Tendril, Ordinary Folk, Giant Ant, Oddfellows, Studio AKA, Gondry, and Elastic/Patrick Clair. Load before starting production on any piece.

## Studio process facts

**Buck** ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2012/03/26/interview-buck-good-books-metamorphosis/), [School of Motion podcast](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/studio-ascended-buck-cofounder-ryan-honey-som-podcast))
- Pitch begins with look frames + written treatment, not boards. Boards come after the look exists.
- The "boardomatic" (timed board edit) is the decision engine: "We figured most things out [there]."
- Technique is chosen per shot AFTER design locks — 3D, pencil, or a few keyframes, per what the frame needs. Hard shots get brutal iteration ("re-animated so many times we stopped counting").
- Kill criteria at intake: creative merit → strategic opportunity → relationship.

**Tendril** ([Motionographer](http://motionographer.com/2013/02/07/in-depth-coverage-stylefames-ny-opener/))
- Story kernel first, in one meeting — a single narrative spine before any visuals.
- Full previs/animatic as the load-bearing document: camera, timing, shot count locked before design polish; parallel tracks (live action, CG, environments) all contract against the animatic.

**Ordinary Folk** ([process page](https://www.ordinaryfolk.co/process), [School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/ordinary-folk-podcast))
- Four stages: Message → Design → Animation → Audio. Script first ("we shut up and listen").
- Strict order: script → storyboard → style frames → per-shot animation. Complex shots: mock the camera move first, build a skeleton, then detail. ~1 week of animation per 10–15 hero seconds.
- Small on purpose; the staleness check kills comfort: "the moment that we know exactly what's going to be... we're getting too comfortable."

**Giant Ant** ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/jay-grandin-podcast-podcast))
- Three questions before any visual work: what should people KNOW, how should they FEEL, what should they DO. Feeling outranks information.
- Design-first conversion: dedicated illustrators set direction via boards + style frames before animation.
- Anti-pitching: 48-hour pitches favor the first idea, not the best one. Their best work existed because they had time "to get it wrong a couple times first."
- Intake filter: Would our mothers be proud? Would we use this? Creative opportunity? Financial opportunity? Have we done this before?

**Oddfellows** ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/tj-kearney-podcast))
- Bids itemized by pipeline artifact (storyboard, style frames, keyframes, animatics) — the client buys process stages explicitly.
- Identity = consistently high execution standard, not a house style.

**Michel Gondry** ([Star Guitar making-of](https://viewinder.com/star-guitar/), [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/05/watch-how-michel-gondry-pulled-off-all-those-insane-effects-white-stripes-videos), [WePresent](https://wepresent.wetransfer.com/stories/idles-gondry-model-village))
- Reverse-engineer the track on graph paper first (Star Guitar: every beat plotted, then translated to a visual blueprint).
- Prototype for pennies before shooting (tested Star Guitar with oranges, shoes, and glasses on a sidewalk).
- Build the set around the effect; refuse post. Hyperballad: rewound film in-camera, re-exposed the same negative seven times "with no idea what it would look like."
- Constraints formalized: Home Movie Factory workshops ban editing entirely. "The smaller the budget is, the more I work."

**Studio AKA** ([Skwigly](https://www.skwigly.co.uk/studio-aka-and-philip-hunt/))
- One diagnostic question opens every brief: laugh, stun, or confuse? — emotional target before craft.
- "As soon as you treat it as art you are sunk" (for commercial work): find the problem, fix it by the simplest principles.
- Personal films are the R&D engine; dead pitches get recycled into art-house flagships (Jo Jo in the Stars).

**Elastic / Patrick Clair** ([Art of the Title — Westworld](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/westworld/), [CreativeFuture](https://www.creativefuture.org/main-title-designer-patrick-clair/))
- The one-sentence kill test: "If you can't sum up your idea in a sentence, then you don't know what it is."
- Written concept + massive reference deck before frames; research is a staffed role (visual researchers sift thousands of images).
- Style frames → storyboards → playblasts with real feedback loops; sound arrives mid-process and reshapes animation (piano-key animation matched to videos of Djawadi's team playing).

## The pipeline: 9 stages, each exists to kill something while it's cheap

| # | Stage | Decision made here | What gets killed here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concept / intake | Should this exist? What's the emotional job (know/feel/do; laugh/stun/confuse) | Wrong-fit ideas, comfortable repeats |
| 2 | Written idea / treatment | The one-sentence core + narrative spine | Concepts that can't compress; style masquerading as story |
| 3 | Reference / mood | Emotional register, palette, artistic lineage | Tonal directions — before any original pixels |
| 4 | Style frames | THE look — one polished frame as aesthetic contract | The competing looks that lose; ambiguity about "done" |
| 5 | Boards / animatic | Sequence, shot count, timing against scratch sound | Beats that drag, redundant shots, structure problems — at board cost |
| 6 | Motion tests | Is the riskiest movement achievable and right? | Techniques that don't hold; over-scoped shots |
| 7 | Production | Per-shot execution + medium | Individual shot approaches (never structure/look — those are locked) |
| 8 | Sound | Score/mix locked to picture, often reshaping timing | Music that competes with the piece |
| 9 | Grade / polish / QA | The final unifying pass; the "try again" loop | The gap between 90% and shippable |

**The through-line:** money and time flow downhill, so decisions are forced uphill. Every artifact (treatment, mood board, style frame, animatic, motion test) is a deliberately cheap fake of the final film, built so a specific class of idea can be killed before it gets expensive. Gondry runs the identical logic with physical prototypes instead of pipeline documents.
