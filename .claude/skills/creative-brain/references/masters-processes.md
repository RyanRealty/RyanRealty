# The Masters' Processes — how great animators actually work

Research date: 2026-07-09. Process facts with sources, not style descriptions. Load when starting any new piece or when a piece feels derivative.

## Don Hertzfeldt (2026 Annecy Cristal, "Paper Trail")

- **Nonlinear, accumulative writing.** Never writes A-to-Z: "I'll usually come up with R one morning and then E in the shower, and then a few weeks later W." Fragments accumulate until connections emerge. World of Tomorrow Ep. 3 was reverse-engineered backward from one climactic image he refused to abandon; a whiteboard timeline made the logic "bulletproof." ([BFI Sight & Sound](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/don-hertzfeldt-world-tomorrow-stick-animation))
- **Found audio as story skeleton.** World of Tomorrow was built AROUND unscripted recordings of his 4-year-old niece; he wrote the sci-fi around her spontaneous lines. "I like to have some fluidity in the thing if I'm going to be working on it for months or years." ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/j5v7d8/don-hertzfeldt-world-of-tomorrow-2-interview))
- **1999–2011 all in-camera** on a 1940s 35mm rostrum camera: split-screens via multiple exposures through holes under the lens, rewinding film, re-exposing. Six years for It's Such a Beautiful Day. "i like paper and pens and paint... work with my hands and build props."
- **Deliberate isolation + deliberate obsolescence.** Works alone for years; production computer kept on 2008 software with no internet so nothing updates or distracts.
- **The audience is the studio.** No commercial work ever; funds each film from direct sales of the last. "Exposure is useless if you never get paid." On grind: "getting through the boring stuff is almost always the difference between a project existing or not existing."
- **Paper Trail (2026):** one person in every key role; new hybrid technique (2D computer animation + animated physical paper objects) invented for the film. "It's not unusual to be sitting in a screening... and something goes by in 10 seconds that took you six months to make." ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/annecy-top-winners-the-violinist-iron-boy-don-hertzfeldt-1236796087/), [Sundance Meet the Artist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DQPJWy4Y1X8))

## Georges Schwizgebel

- **Fixed hierarchy: music → movement → visuals → story.** Story explicitly last. Films are continuous flows, scenes melt through metamorphosis. ([Zippy Frames interview](https://www.zippyframes.com/interviews/georges-schwizgebel-interview))
- **Score commissioned at storyboard stage with tempo specified** (told his composer: 90bpm for the black-and-white passage, 120bpm for the dance) — beat structure locked before painting begins.
- **Pencil first, then acrylic on cel, no software.** Darwin's Notebook ≈ 5,000 paintings shot on twos, one year of painting. Delegates only compositing.
- **Continuous transforming shots planned as geometric loops.** Battle of San Romano: divided Uccello's painting into 16 sections, moved through them in a counter-clockwise spiral over a 36-painting cycle ending where it began. "The hardest part was at the beginning, when I had to decide which element of the image was going to turn into which other element." ([Vimeo blog](https://vimeo.com/blog/post/the-battle-of-san-romano-georges-schwizgebel))
- **Ideas gestate for a decade** (saw the Darwin exhibition ~10 years before starting the film).

## Norman McLaren

- **The doctrine: design the invisible.** "Animation is not the art of drawings that move but the art of movements that are drawn... what happens between each frame is much more important than what exists on each frame." ([Art UK](https://artuk.org/discover/stories/drawing-movement-norman-mclarens-experiments-in-animation))
- **Sound was drawn, not recorded.** Inked directly on the 35mm optical soundtrack; card index of hand-drawn wave patterns spanning five octaves; pitch specifiable to a tenth of a tone. Image and sound share one physical substrate — sync is structural, not added in post. ([NFB — Pen Point Percussion](https://www.nfb.ca/film/pen_point_percussion/))
- **Wrote technical notes on every film for 51 years** (1933–1984), archived by the NFB. The lab notebook was part of the practice. ([NFB Technical Notes PDF](https://www3.nfb.ca/archives_mclaren/notech/NT_EN.pdf))
- **One new constraint per film.** Each film exists to exhaust one technique, then write it up and move on.

## Jan Švankmajer (Decalogue — [mendel.earth archive](https://www.mendel.earth/archive1999-2006/eng/text/decalog.htm))

- **Trust the body over the eye.** "Touch is an older sense than eyesight"; the eye is "tired and spoilt." Texture is content.
- **Interrogate the object before animating it.** "Animation isn't moving about inert things but their revival." Old objects carry imprints of the people who touched them. "Use animation as a magical operation."
- **The deeper the fantasy, the more pedantic the realism.** Rule 6: impossible premises survive only on obsessive concrete detail.
- **The script is a safety net, not the work.** Rule 10: creative process outranks the idea. Rule 2: "Succumb totally to your obsessions. You have nothing better anyway."

## Studio Ghibli / Miyazaki

- **Production starts before the script exists.** Begins with a hazy idea and a single image, then draws e-konte (storyboards) directly — no screenplay. The Boy and the Heron: 603 pages of boards in place of any text script. ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/865373/studio-ghiblis-scripting-process-is-practically-nonexistent/))
- **Timing is written on the board in seconds.** Every e-konte cut carries duration; margins carry dialogue, sound, camera, emotional direction. The board IS script + edit + shot design. ([Discover Ghibli](https://discoverghibli.com/what-are-storyboards-or-ekonte/))
- **One authority corrects every frame-defining drawing.** Miyazaki personally reviews and corrects layouts, erases-and-redraws details that ring false.
- **"Ma" is scheduled, not accidental.** "It's there intentionally... If you just have non-stop action with no breathing space at all, it's just busyness." Pauses are budgeted screen time. ([RogerEbert.com](https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/hayao-miyazaki-interview))

## Recent Annecy winners

- **Percebes (2024 Cristal):** real recorded testimony as the soundtrack spine; watercolor + salt chosen because the film is about the sea; palette constrained to three colors. Material argues the theme. ([Animation Magazine](https://www.animationmagazine.net/2024/09/percebes-directors-alexandra-ramires-laura-goncalves-discuss-their-annecy-cristal-winning-short/))
- **The Night Boots (2025 Cristal):** pinscreen chosen BEFORE the script because the story is nocturnal — "the technique guided the writing." Writing started from two bookend images; the middle was invented afterward. One year alone in a dark room. ([Animation Scoop](https://www.animationscoop.com/interview-stepping-into-the-night-boots/))

## The 10 transferable process laws

1. **Choose the constraint before the story, then let the constraint write.** A hard formal limit is the idea engine.
2. **Lock the temporal spine first — usually with sound.** Never animate first and "add audio in post."
3. **Board it, don't script it — write the seconds on the board.** The timed board IS the screenplay, edit, and schedule.
4. **Write nonlinearly from a fragment bank.** Keep a standing pile of fragments; assembly is the writing.
5. **Design the movement, not the drawings.** Judge work by how it moves, never by how a still looks.
6. **Make the material argue the theme.** Texture is content — never fake a surface you could actually use.
7. **The deeper the fantasy, the more pedantic the realism.** Impossible premises need airtight internal logic.
8. **Schedule emptiness (ma).** Non-stop action is just busyness. Put the pauses in the timing sheet.
9. **One owner per frame, tiny team, boring tools.** Concentrate creative authority; strip the toolchain.
10. **Persist on drudgery economics and write down your process.** 10 seconds can cost six months; document every technique the moment you invent it so it compounds (McLaren's 51 years of notes).
