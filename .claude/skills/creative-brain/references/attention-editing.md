# Attention & Editing — the science of the frame, the cut, and the beat

Research date: 2026-07-09. How fast frames are read, what captures gaze, and how editing controls collective attention. Load when designing hooks, boards, cuts, and pacing.

## The findings

**1. A frame's gist is read in 13-100ms (High).** Concept detection at 13ms/picture (Potter's RSVP, [MIT](https://news.mit.edu/2014/in-the-blink-of-an-eye-0116)); 75%-correct scene categorization at 19-67ms, global properties (layout, mood, natural/man-made) BEFORE object identity (Greene & Oliva).
→ Frame 1 communicates concept + mood at gist speed: one dominant subject, clean figure-ground, no fine print at t=0.

**2. Faces are the strongest gaze magnet; text second (High).** Faces fixated ~16.6× and text ~11.1× more than control regions; a face is hit within the first two fixations >80% of the time ([Cerf, Frady & Koch](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2122098)). Raw saliency (Itti & Koch contrast/color/orientation) predicts ~53%. Motion ONSET (not steady motion) captures attention involuntarily (Franconeri & Simons). Center bias dominates the first 1-2s of any shot (Tatler).
→ Use a face/eyes to place the eye; never let a face and floating text compete; animate important elements IN (onset); center the subject on early beats.

**3. Editing evolved toward the rhythm of attention (High).** Cutting's 150-film corpus: shot lengths drifted toward 1/f (pink noise) fluctuations matching attention's natural rhythm — clustered fast beats, then slow, not metronomic. 75-year drift: "quicker, faster, darker" (ASL from ~10s to low single digits, r=−.75) ([Cutting 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797610361679)).
→ Vary pacing in clustered, self-similar bursts; lean modern (shorter shots, more motion, contrast subject-on-dark).

**4. Neurocinematics: authorship is measurable neural control (High).** Hasson's inter-subject correlation: Hitchcock synchronized >65% of viewers' cortex; Leone ~45%; Curb ~18%; an unedited park shot <5% ([Neurocinematics 2008](https://www.motionpictures.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Hasson-etal_NeuroCinematics2008.pdf)).
→ "Nice footage, no editing" is the scientific failure mode. Author every beat; deliberate structure IS what makes an audience respond as one.

**5. Cuts hide behind action (High).** Match-action cuts missed 32.4% vs 9.4% for between-scene cuts; a third missed when the cut lands on a motion onset (Smith's Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity). Viewers hold no detailed frame model (invisible gorilla: ~50% miss it; 83% believe they'd catch changes, ~11% do).
→ Cut on motion onsets with the attended subject continuous — swap scenes/assets invisibly. Spend budget ONLY where the viewer looks; economize everywhere else.

**6. Audiences blink in sync at narrative breakpoints (High).** Nakano: spontaneous blinks synchronize at implicit story breakpoints (beat resolution); blinks micro-activate the DMN — the audience rests where the author provides a lull ([Proc R Soc B](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/276/1673/3635/72874/)).
→ Engineer micro-lulls at act breaks to reset attention; NEVER place the critical stat or logo reveal on a natural breakpoint (that's where eyes close).

**7. The psychological present is ~2-3s; memory encodes event boundaries (High).** Pöppel's subjective present; Zacks' Event Segmentation Theory: boundaries register where prediction fails (changes in motion, space, character, goal) and boundaries are remembered better than middles.
→ Build ~2-4s beats, each a clean event (one location, one subject, one micro-goal); put the thing that must be remembered AT an engineered boundary.

**8. The forward push-in is the most visceral move — dose it (Medium-High).** Expanding optic flow (vection) creates felt self-motion and presence; oversustained/fast → nausea on a phone.
→ Short forward bursts on hook and payoff only.

## The attention and editing laws (ranked)

1. First frame readable at gist speed — concept + mood in 100ms.
2. Faces then text are the gaze magnets; never let them compete.
3. Author every beat — editing is neural control (65% vs 5%).
4. Chunk into 2-4s events; land the memorable thing on a boundary.
5. Introduce anything important with a motion onset.
6. Hide cuts behind action (match-action + onset = invisible seams).
7. Pace in 1/f clusters, never on a metronome.
8. Modern grammar: shorter, more motion, contrast subject-on-dark.
9. Spend only where the viewer looks; the corners are invisible.
10. Forward push-in for hook and payoff, in short doses.
11. Center-frame early beats (center bias owns the first 2 seconds).
12. Lulls at act breaks; critical info never on a breakpoint.
