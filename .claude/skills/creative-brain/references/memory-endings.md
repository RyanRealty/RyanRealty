# Memory, Endings & Story Shapes — what sticks and how to end

Research date: 2026-07-09. Load when designing endings, story arcs, brand placement, and anything that must be remembered.

## The findings

**1. Peak-end rule (High lab; Moderate media extension).** Experiences are remembered by their PEAK and their END; duration is neglected (Kahneman/Redelmeier colonoscopy + cold-pressor studies — people chose to REPEAT the objectively longer-but-better-ending trial). Media extension (Unruly/Affectiva/Shotton): an emotional peak WHILE the brand is on screen doubled brand recall (10% → 21%); end-peak ads hit 43% ad recall vs 23% control.
→ One clear emotional peak with the brand present; the last 3-5 seconds are the film's second-best moment. Never end on a flat logo card. The middle can sag.

**2. Emotional arousal consolidates memory — but boosts confidence, not accuracy (High).** McGaugh: arousal → noradrenaline in the basolateral amygdala → stronger consolidation. Flashbulb caveat (Talarico & Rubin): vividness and certainty stay high while accuracy decays like normal memory.
→ Attach the brand to a FEELING; don't expect a spoken statistic inside a high-arousal moment to survive — put numbers on screen and hold them.

**3. Picture superiority (High).** Standing 1973: >90% recognition across 10,000 pictures; words lag badly. (Mechanism contested — distinctiveness vs dual coding — effect is not.)
→ Carry the message in imagery; words are labels.

**4. Von Restorff isolation (High effect; Moderate uplift figures).** The different item is the remembered item. Distinctive brand assets: +34% ad recognition (Romaniuk); +62% short-term profit ROI across ~1,300 campaigns (Ebiquity).
→ Every film carries the same unmistakable ownable device (navy-cream world, Jax, a motion signature). Sameness is forgettable by design.

**5. Self-reference effect (High, meta-analytically robust).** Material encoded relative to the SELF is remembered best (Rogers, Kuiper & Kirker 1977).
→ "You/your" as the subject isn't just brand voice — it's an encoding advantage. The film is about their move, not the brokerage.

**6. Six story shapes (Moderate-High).** Reagan/Dodds 2016 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07772)): 1,737 stories collapse into six emotional arcs; most successful = turn-based shapes (Icarus, Oedipus) and CHAINED arcs (two sequential man-in-a-hole; Cinderella-then-tragedy). Vonnegut vindicated.
→ Default for 45s: man in a hole (problem → turn → resolution, brand as the turn). Highest-engagement register: chain two small fall-then-rise beats.

**7. Suspense/curiosity/surprise are edit decisions (High).** Brewer & Lichtenstein's structural-affect theory: the same events in different reveal ORDER produce different emotions. Suspense = initiating event shown, outcome postponed. Curiosity = key earlier event withheld. Surprise = concealed then revealed. Gerrig's anomalous suspense: suspense survives re-watching (memory doesn't pre-fetch known outcomes) — reruns aren't wasted retention.
→ Emotion is free: it's the order of reveal, not the budget.

**8. Open loops (Moderate — shaky replication, strong industry signal).** Zeigarnik tension: incomplete tasks stay cognitively active.
→ End open ONLY when there's a payoff to reach (a question answered on the site); unresolved-with-nowhere-to-go reads as a broken ad. Full closure when the job is a warm complete brand feeling.

**9. Humor's vampire effect (Moderate).** Funny lifts attention and liking, but detached humor drains the brand — viewers keep the gag, lose the name; worst in trust-driven categories.
→ If humor: the brand must BE the joke, same beat, not gag-then-logo.

**10. THE CENTRAL TENSION: peak-end vs primacy (Moderate-High).** Naive peak-end says save the brand for last; the effectiveness data says brand cue in the FIRST 2 SECONDS or the creator/film gets recalled instead of the brand. Reconciliation: **the end owns emotional memory; the opening + persistent distinctive assets own which brand gets credited.**
→ The winning structure: ownable brand cue at the open (doubles as the thumbnail), distinctive asset woven throughout, brand present at the emotional peak, strong ending. Never a beautiful 40s story that names the brand at second 42. (Note: for pure art-house pieces the "brand cue" is the WORLD itself — navy-cream, Jax, the style — not a logo; that's the whole point of owning a distinctive world.)

## The memory and story laws (ranked)

1. Brand early, brand throughout, own the end (world-as-brand counts).
2. One emotional peak with the brand present.
3. The ending is the second-best moment; never a flat logo card.
4. Show it in pictures; label with words.
5. Be unmistakably different from every other real-estate ad.
6. Talk to "you"; tell the viewer's story.
7. Attach the brand to a feeling; put facts on screen, not in the peak.
8. Control emotion through reveal order, not budget.
9. Man-in-a-hole is the default 45s arc; chain two dips for max engagement.
10. Open a loop only when there's a payoff to reach.
11. If humor, the brand IS the joke.
12. Suspense survives re-watching; re-serves aren't wasted.
