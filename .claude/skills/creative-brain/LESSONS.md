# LESSONS — the empirical ledger

Append-only. Every creative session that learns something adds it here with a date. This is McLaren's 51 years of technical notes, for us. A lesson that lives only in chat history is a lesson we will pay for twice.

## 2026-07-09 — Session 1 (the founding batch)

1. **Models regress to the nearest training cliché of your idea.** "A line draws itself" → Veo produced a photorealistic hand holding a marker (the YouTube whiteboard-explainer trope) despite "invisible pen, no hands" in prompt AND negative prompt. Fix: never name trope-adjacent framings ("draws itself", "pen") — describe the phenomenon abstractly ("the line grows like a living thread"), or don't use a model at all.
2. **Veo negative prompts are weak on nouns you also mention positively.** Saying "no hands" appears to have INDUCED the hand. Don't mention the thing you don't want; describe only what you want.
3. **Never bake typography into a frame a model will animate.** Kling mutated the heritage lettering into gibberish ("PUECRAE · TILT"). Type goes in post, always. Same for any number that must be exact.
4. **Kling 2.5 style-drift can be a gift.** Asked for flat navy-ink-on-cream morph; delivered a sculptural porcelain elk exhaling smoke in a misty forest — better than the brief. Log drifts; some are new directions. But when the constraint IS the piece (One Line), drift = death.
5. **Hailuo 2.3 t2v is 16:9 only.** Portrait requires a first_frame_image anchor.
6. **For absolute formal constraints, animate in code.** The one-line film's truest execution is parametric (PIL/Remotion stroke-reveal): exact path, exact timing, zero AI sheen. AI adds nothing to a constraint piece except drift. Code animation IS an art-house technique (McLaren's direct control, digitally).
7. **I skipped the style-frame stage and paid for it.** One Line v1/v2 went straight from idea to production; the pine read as scribble and the far ridge as a stock chart. The studio pipeline exists for a reason: iterate the STILL until it's gallery-grade, then animate. A weak drawing animated is a weak film.
8. **Check asset provenance before animating a photo.** hero-old-mill-master-4k.jpg is frame 1 of a stock VIDEO (iStock-1330945786) — animating it regenerated motion that already exists as licensable footage. Filmable = license/shoot, never generate.
9. **Researching "AI video trends" produces derivative work by construction.** The trend lane (Cakeify/Squish/Melting/Ghibli-filter) is the slop lane. Taste sources: Annecy, AIFF, festival shorts, art history. Matt's exact words: "LOOK FOR TRUE ART HOUSES."
10. **Replicate ops:** predictions POST can hang (use --max-time / urllib timeouts + retry on 429); prediction JSON contains control chars (parse with strict=False); heredoc temp files written in a timed-out Bash call may not persist — write files via Python, not heredocs, for critical steps.
11. **THE REALITY LAW (Matt, verbatim: "I don't want real life items converted using AI, it's literally the definition of slop").** The porcelain elk — our ranked-1 clip — was killed at doctrine level: it renders real-life items (elk, forest) photoreally via AI. Craft quality is irrelevant; simulating reality IS the slop. The permitted lane is authored stylized worlds only (heritage engraving universe, line art, designed abstraction) where AI supplies motion to OUR drawings. This also killed chrome-rain, folding-house, and infinite-descent. Survivors of batch 001: the heritage world clip and the One Line direction. I mis-ranked the batch by judging craft instead of authorship-of-world — the kill tests now check reality FIRST.
12. **THE BANKNOTE PRINCIPLE (Matt: "we need to show complexity").** Simple line art alone reads as low-effort. The bar: gist-fluent macro (reads in 100ms) + virtuosic micro (thousands of authored elements). Lineage: Doré, banknote guilloché, wimmelbild. Empirical: v1 of Ten Thousand Currents failed — free particles everywhere at low alpha render as pencil dust/speckle, complexity WITHOUT gestalt. v2 passed by building three authored SYSTEMS (contour-echo mountain, ink-filament river channel, sparse wind currents) — complexity must be organized INTO the composition's structures, not scattered across the frame. Also: filament trails need length (25+ points) and real ink alpha (60+) or they read grey.
13. **Working stack verified 2026-07-09:** Veo 3.1 + 3.1 Fast (native audio, i2v, 1080p), Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro (i2v respects start_image aspect), Hailuo 2.3, Seedance 1 Pro (t2v aspect_ratio incl. 9:16, up to 1080p), Wan 2.5. Kling 3.0 NOT on Replicate yet. Sora discontinued 2026. Costs observed: Veo 3.1 Fast 8s/720p ≈ $1-2; full batch of 6 generations ≈ $10-15.
14. **SESSION HALT (Matt: "this is worse than the last one, just forget it").** Ten Thousand Currents failed his bar too. The honest reading: doctrine is not the gap anymore — my execution taste is. Two consecutive pieces I judged as passing were rejected on sight. Until the internal judge is recalibrated, nothing gets shown that isn't unambiguously at the level of the best work in the reference volumes, and when the principal says stop, stop — no counter-pitch.

## 2026-07-10 — Beaumont sold carousel (produce-mode)
- `listing_photos` can be EMPTY for closed listings — the reliable photo source is `listings.details->'Photos'` jsonb (Spark CDN `Uri1600`), gated by `media_suppressed`.
- The `reviews` table goes stale (was 32 days old; the actual latest GBP review had posted THE SAME DAY). Any "latest review" deliverable must run `scripts/ingest-gbp-reviews.mjs --apply --replace` first — `--apply` alone skips when rows exist.
- Landscape→portrait smart-crop centered on x kills off-center focal points (lit fireplace cut from frame). `focus_x` param added to `build_beaumont_sold_carousel.py`; inspect every cropped interior before surfacing.

## 2026-08-14 — Reality Law retired

15. **THE REALITY LAW IS RETIRED (Matt: those rules are prohibitive; we are better at this now).** Lesson #11 stays as history of why unconstrained photoreal failed in July (porcelain elk, prompt-only Grok banners, "Ratas Rales"). It is no longer a kill-on-sight ban on rendering real places. The live standard is SKILL law 1: reference-conditioned Central Oregon place work is allowed; prompt-only scenic slop is refuse; do not invent a listing, a room, or the view from an address; do not generate people as residents; charts and numbers render in code. PLAYBOOK "reality test" is now the reference test.

## 2026-08-16 — xAI studio, NorthWest Crossing stills

16. **Imagine keeps or mutates street signs from the source drone.** Four of ten NWX takes passed the navy sat gate and still died on letters in the frame ("NW Crossing Dr", "Mt Washington Dr", garbled Washington). The sat gate does not read type. After every place Make, look at the island and the posts. Readable letters are a kill. Do not ask the model to remove the sign — that induces the sign (lesson 2). Crop tighter or pick a still without chrome letters.
17. **Cream-in-the-trees can drift infrared-white.** Asking for cream left in the trees made deciduous masses glow like an IR plate. The mute still passed sat. Watch the foliage: dusty midtone, not a white flare.
18. **Walker-and-dog is promptable; the dog is not guaranteed.** h4 and s5 landed both. s3 landed a walker and dropped the dog. If the life beat is the hang, kill the take that loses the dog rather than calling the silhouette enough.
19. **Geo tags lie.** Area Guide 02 is tagged northwest-crossing and is Sisters downtown. Area Guide 04 is tagged as a river/canal and is Discovery Park from across the pond. Visual-verify before IMAGE_0. Discovery Pond is irrigation — do not send a fish rise.
20. **i2v animates the nearest readable ground, and one subject only.** Hung NWX still `v8-walk` is from the outer walk: the bottom of the frame is walk + brick apron, the asphalt ring is mid-frame. Stacking car + balloon + walker in 8s made a three-act play the model cannot stage — car rode the brick, balloon sat on the sculpture (tallest object under the sky), second beat overlapped. Fix: lock the still, one living thing per take. Balloon must already be high in open sky. A walker belongs on this still; a car has to hit the mid-frame ring or it will use the apron.
21. **v8b empirical (same still, five singles, 10s).** `walk-dog` hung: person and dog on the outer walk, left to right, paint held. `bike-walk` same ground, useful. `sky-balloon` left the top of the frame (first balloon that actually exits) but sat centered over the sculpture mid-clip. `breathe` proved the painting can live with only clouds and grain. `car-ring` still put the car on the brick and invented a second Mini at the end. A car through this circle needs a hang still whose foreground is the asphalt, not this walk view.
22. **Do not put the wordmark in a generated frame.** Matt asked for the logo on the balloon. v8e failed on sight: the composite sat as a flat paste at t=0, the second line was mush by mid-clip, and the paint went heavier than the locked stand. I extracted frames and still hung it. That is a judge failure, not a missing-eyes failure. Hang stays `nwx-v8d-stand.mp4`. Type in post. The balloon stays empty cloth.

## 2026-09-23 — Story films: "Winter, 1982" (vintage visitors #1)

23. **Ask the generator for period CONTENT; own the LOOK in the lab.** Clean "candid photograph, 1982" prompts beat "Super 8 frame" prompts (same period read, more natural skin), and one film-lab pass (`scripts/studio/filmlab.py`) makes eleven generations one reel of one stock. Model-baked grain differs take to take.
24. **Every era cue is scene content.** A shared "brick storefronts" cue put a downtown main street under the ski run and on the road to Bachelor; "full moustaches" put a moustache on the woman in 2 of 3 cast takes; "people look into the lens and wave" made them wave mid-toast. Beats declare which cues they need (`periodCues`), hair is per person, the camera line carries no action, and vehicles are conditional ("any car in frame is...").
25. **Period wardrobe drifts a decade unless named exactly.** "Color-blocked one-piece" came back 1990s teal-and-magenta. 1982 = solid colors with one chest stripe, bibs, matte nylon, straight skis with no sidecut.
26. **Reference stills leak their text.** The 1987 bus-depot photo put "BEND'S BIGGEST SHOPPING CENTER" into a night street; the Wall St photo put "WE FOREST US" on a pole. Storefront beats will always carry signs; move the lifestyle beat somewhere signless (Drake Park path) rather than fight it. The Drake Park reference carried the real logging wheels into frame, which locals will recognize.
27. **Continuity sources carry people and props with them.** Handing the stroll still to the sign beat put the couple beside the sign (better: the vacation pose); handing the sign still to the phone and call beats put the blank sign behind them (better again: sign + phone + call in one frame, tracked composite).
28. **The judge is strict on details the screen will not show.** grok-4.7 flagged mitten fusion, a thumbnail-sized shoulder emblem, and boot styling. Curation is authorship: override with `--note`, written into the manifest. It also correctly killed shaped skis, glossy puffers, a moustache on the woman, and a modern selfie arm.
29. **The brand never goes through a generator, and a sign is a composite.** Real sign art warped onto a blank panel: a still + code camera for the reveal, ECC-homography tracking with a chroma occlusion key for moving clips (his shoulder and thumbs-up stay in front). Hand-placed corners beat auto-detection when snow and sky share the panel's brightness.
30. **Cut to the score's grid.** ElevenLabs Music at 104.00 bpm: every cut on a beat, the projector stops and the phone appears exactly on the downbeat at 25.40s, the final chord lands on the end card. Digital silence at the break read as a glitch; a whisper of room tone makes it a hush.
31. **Production ops.** A judge timeout crashed a stage and a whole-object merge orphaned in-flight takes (four paid clips vanished from the record, files safe on disk). Saves now merge in place per take, a judge failure keeps the take unjudged, and `adopt` recovers anything on disk the manifest lost. The xAI team spending limit ended generation mid-piece; check headroom before a batch.
32. **Cost is a craft constraint (Matt 2026-09-23: "be more fiscally responsible").** The first piece cost ~$30 against an ~$8 rate-card estimate; the gap was the grok-4.7 judge on every still with reference sheets attached and/or 1080p video. None of it showed on screen: the lab resolves below 480p, no trim used more than 3s of a 6s clip, and I overrode the judge on half the picks anyway. Defaults are now 480p, 4s, 2 takes, judge opt-in, contact sheets for curation, raw ticks on every ledger line. Creative work should bill to its own xAI team: one session emptied the shared prepaid balance and blocked production (CRM reply intent, the SMS agent, CMA).
33. **A reference account is a register, not a template.** Studio Shibuya's vibe is composed deadpan framing, a signature-look character, brand-as-in-world-merch, and a measurable muted pastel stock. Encode the measurable part (the `cine_pastel` stock, the tripod grammar); invent our own character and merch.

## 2026-09-23 — "Winter, 1982" v2 (Matt's notes on the pastel cut)

34. **A full-frame app takeover reads as an ad; the anachronism belongs inside the film.** Matt called the phone break "lame". What replaced it: the phone held up to the lens inside the 1982 footage, with a blank lit screen in the generated frame and our page laid onto it AFTER the lab, so the screen is the only sharp, ungraded thing in the shot. The joke is the one modern object in the frame, not a cut to a website.
35. **Deadpan beats a thumbs-up.** "Don't make it corny at the end." The last shot is a centered tripod tableau with straight faces, phone at his ear, our sign on the lawn. Music stops dead on the cut; one ring in the hush; a pickup click; then the card. No tagline. Silence is the punchline.
36. **Generators copy a reference's lettering.** The 2012 Tower photo's marquee copy ("THE PRODUCERS") came back on the 1982 marquee. Any lettered surface in a reference gets plated in post with period-true copy, verified like a figure (On Golden Pond: #1 nationally Feb 5-7, 1982).
37. **Lens length is how you make a landmark read.** At 35mm the Tower was a postage stamp behind the couple; asking for 85mm compressing the street put the marquee large and centered between their profiles. Plate lettering on a 2x-upscaled clip, then let the lab downsample, so the letters stay crisp.
38. **"One following the other" needs depth staging and a place to keep.** Side-on framing gave side-by-side skiers; up the fall line at 85mm gave single file, but motion still let them swap places until the action said he "keeps his place a few meters behind her, following her exact line".
39. **Book the bill, not the card.** xAI's cost_in_usd_ticks is 1e10 to the dollar: reference-conditioned stills bill ~$0.075 (card $0.04), a 4s 480p clip $0.33. The v2 reshoot of four shots cost $2.73. The piece cap now trips on billed spend.
40. **"Make it interesting" meant life in the frame, not more cuts.** Matt's note on v2: après with friends, a black Lab in the car. The dog opens the reel (licking her cheek as she waves: a face and a dog inside two seconds), friends give the one big group laugh mid-reel, and the same dog sits deadpan between them in the last frame. A recurring animal is a callback the viewer earns; one line of prompt ("a young black Labrador retriever with a red collar") held it across generations.
41. **Extras default to clones of the lead.** Asked for "another couple (a man and a woman)", Imagine twice gave two moustached men who looked like our lead. Naming the friends outright (a blonde woman in a red snowflake sweater, her bearded husband in a grey turtleneck) fixed it on the first try. Describe every extra you need by what makes them different from the cast.
42. **A recurring animal needs a reference photo, not a description.** "A young black Labrador retriever with a red collar" held the breed but not the dog; the Drake Park shot also carried a stranger's shaggy dog that read as theirs. Matt: "the dog is different in each section." A frontal crop of the Lab from a finished frame, registered in the asset library and passed like a cast sheet (`StoryPiece.companion`), made it one dog across three new shots on the first try.
43. **Show the sign the moment they see the house.** Cutting from her pointing to a sign close-up made the house appear sign-less first. Put the blank panel in the pointing shot and plate it; the close-up then goes, and the continuity chain runs from the shot where the sign is first seen. A continuity still also drags its people along: the sign close-up regenerated her and the dog from the pointing frame.

## 2026-09-24 — "Winter, 1982" v7: the homecoming

44. **The Bend story ends at home, not at the sign.** Matt: people vacation here, go home, and realize what they are doing does not compare. The call moved from the lawn to their own kitchen a week later, on the phone they own. A second arc (`HOMECOMING_ARC`), not a longer visitor arc: the trip, the leaving, the city, the call.
45. **Change the register to show what they went back to.** Handheld warm home movie for the trip; locked-off, square, grey tableaux for the city (Tati's Playtime, Roy Andersson). The snapshot from Bend is the only saturated thing in the office frames: grey the plate around it at composite time, not in the stock, or the stock greys the photo too.
46. **A photo inside a frame is a composite, like a sign.** Ask for a brass frame holding a plain blank white card; lay a real frame of the trip on it. The same chairlift selfie on both desks (doubles) is a rhyme the viewer gets without a word.
47. **Legibility is a zoom.** A phone number on a sign at 12% of frame width is a smear after the lab. The amateur power zoom into the frame she pointed in (a `plateFrom` beat, the still composited at 4x) puts it at 44% and costs nothing to generate.
48. **Stills set the expression the motion starts from.** "Looks at the photo on his desk" came back smiling at work in all eight takes; "tired and frowning, holds the telephone, then looks at the frame" gave the stress-to-longing turn the scene is for.
49. **Name the one prop and where its cord goes.** The kitchen call came back with a cream handset in his hand and a gold wall phone with its own handset behind him. "One harvest-gold rotary wall telephone, the only telephone in the room, its coiled cord running up to the base beside his head" fixed it.
50. **Continuity sources carry people, again (see 27).** The packing still handed to the rear-window shot put the couple and skiers standing on the road behind a moving car. No continuity plus "the empty road" came back clean in one of four; i2v then froze the road, and a second motion take moved it.
51. **Locked off means static; a warm card is not neutral.** ECC tracking a desk frame or a table card drifted 282px and 1379px as people moved near it. Hold the quad still on a tripod shot, and key the occlusion on the card's own colour, because a white card under a 2800K lamp is yellow.
52. **"My phone number" has two true answers.** `brokers.twilio_number` (the business line, rings through to Matt's cell) and `forward_to_cell` (the cell on his printed signs). A public video carries the business line; say which and why, and keep the swap to one re-render.

## 2026-09-24 — "April, 1982": the Polaroid cut

53. **"Facing outwards" is the default the model draws.** Asked for a photo on a desk, it turns the photo and the person to the lens. Write the camera behind them ("only the backs of their heads", "his face turned away from the camera") or in profile with the photo angled between them; "over her shoulder" alone came back with her smiling back at us.
54. **A generator will draw on a blank card during motion.** Blank in the still, a man in a suit by 0.8s. Key the composite on clean frames of the card (anything matching none of them is in front of it), or cover the whole quad when nothing crosses it.
55. **A card in a hand needs a tracker that follows, not one that looks for the brightest thing.** The screen finder found the card in 43 of 121 frames (a cream blouse and a beige phone competed); following the nearest white blob and holding the shape while fingers cover it found every frame.
56. **Slow is a number.** v7 averaged 2.8s a shot and Matt called it too fast; the Polaroid cut holds 3.2-4.4s, and dissolves only at the time jumps.
57. **A continuity source carries the people again (27, 50).** The house snapshot generated from the discover still came back with the couple and the dog in it; "the sidewalk and the lawn empty, nobody on the lawn or the sidewalk" gave an empty yard.
58. **A real summit photo exists if you look past the first page.** Commons had summit shots without the Sisters; Openverse found the Forest Service's public-domain photo from the top of Bachelor with South Sister and Broken Top. Register it before generating.
59. **One word ages a dog.** The drive-up shot had no companion reference and its props said "a young black Labrador"; Matt saw a puppy. Every shot with the dog carries the reference photo, and nothing in a prompt gives it an age.
60. **An identity reference of two people puts both people in the shot.** Handing the couple's photo to his commute put her in the passenger seat. A one-person shot gets a crop of that person only.
61. **"Epic" references can turn the Cascades Alpine.** A winter Broken Top photo made every peak a jagged spire. Name each volcano's real shape (South Sister a broad smooth cone, Broken Top a broken crater rim) and use a long lens to stack them big behind the people.
62. **When the song is picked in the app, put the radio click at the top.** Instagram starts the song at 0:00; a radio switched on at 0.3s means it lines up with no timeline editing. Drop looped beds (the projector) under a song so nothing repeats against it.
