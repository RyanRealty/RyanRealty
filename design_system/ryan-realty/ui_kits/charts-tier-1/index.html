01
Seller concessions

The concession didn't get bigger. It got normal.

Ten thousand dollars has been the median concession for seven straight quarters. What changed is how many sellers pay one: 37.0% of closings in 2024 Q1, 47.9% so far in 2026 Q3. A seller pricing off gross comps is budgeting for a cost that now lands on roughly half of all sales.

Why this starts at 2024. The concessions flag is only fully populated from 2024-01-01: 2024, 2025, 2026 all carry it on 100% of closings, against 73.7% in 2023 and nothing usable before. An earlier start would read as a rising trend that is really rising reporting. Gross and net medians are taken over the same 968-row set — seller net is non-null for every row in this window, which is not true of the pre-2024 data.
02
Sale to original ask

Redmond gave back a point. Bend picked one up.

Measured against the original asking price, not the last one — so every price cut along the way stays in the number. Redmond sellers closed at 98.76% of their opening ask last quarter, down 1.15 points from 99.91% a year earlier. Bend went the other way, 97.79% to 98.46%.

03
Days to offer

The MLS clock says 59 days. The offer came on day 23.

Days-on-market keeps counting through inspection, appraisal and financing — work that happens after a buyer has already committed. Time-to-offer stops the clock the day the listing goes pending, which is the only date a seller can actually influence. In Bend the two differ by 36 days, so the number the market quotes is 2.6× the real wait.

04
Price-cut behaviour

Cutting is a resort-community habit, not a Bend one.

6.63% of Bend's 472 active listings are carrying a price cut, below the regional 8.49%. Black Butte Ranch runs at 19.35% — 2.3x the regional rate — on 31 actives and 11.6 months of supply. This is a live snapshot of what is on the market right now, a different population from the closed sales in the three sections above.
