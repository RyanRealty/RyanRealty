Choosing between renting and buying in Bend is arithmetic, and most people miscount two things: the true monthly price of owning, and how many years you need to stay before owning pulls ahead. Here is how we work through it at Ryan Realty LLC, using public figures instead of guesses.

What a rental costs

The federal housing department publishes fair market rents every year. For the Bend-Redmond area in fiscal 2026, they are $1,371 for one bedroom, $1,784 for two bedrooms, and $2,481 for three bedrooms. These figures sit toward the lower-middle of local rents, so plenty of actual houses rent for more. We use the three-bedroom number because that is the kind of home most buyers are weighing. For a specific house, your real rental quote is the number that counts.

What owning costs

Start with $733,000, the recent median single-family sale in Bend. Put 20% down and finance the rest at 6.71%. Principal and interest comes to $3,788 a month. County property tax adds about $426. Then there is upkeep, the line renters tend to forget. A common planning habit is to set aside one percent of the home's value each year, which works out to $611 a month here.

Add it up and the full monthly cost of owning is near $4,825. That is before insurance, which depends on the address because wildfire exposure differs from one property to the next. Get a real quote and add it in.

So what does the monthly comparison say? Owning costs far more than a three-bedroom rental at $2,481. On cash flow, the rental is cheaper in Bend today. Owning has to win a different contest.

What owning gives back

Part of each payment is principal, and that comes back to you when you sell. On this loan, the balance drops by $6,298 in the first year and $36,158 across five.

Then there is appreciation. Bend values climbed sharply between 2020 and 2022 and have been largely level since, so we do not assume any particular rate going forward. Our advice is to run your plan at zero appreciation first. If it only works when prices rise, that is speculation.

The tax deduction deserves caution too. Interest and property tax only help if you itemize, and only the portion above the standard deduction produces a real saving. Check with a tax preparer before counting it.

The break-even

The monthly gap between owning and renting here is about $2,344 before insurance. Layer on the cost of buying and eventually selling, then credit the principal you recover. With zero appreciation, owning does not catch up on cash alone for several years. Add even modest appreciation and the picture shifts fast, because price growth applies to the entire house while a renter's savings only apply to the difference.

That is the core of it. The answer depends on how long you will stay and what you believe about Bend prices over the next stretch.

When renting makes sense

If you are new to town and have not lived through a winter here, rent first. If your job or household could change within a few years, rent. If the payment only works when rates drop or a bonus lands, rent. And if you have not yet looked at the east side or Redmond, look before deciding, because the same money buys more there.

When buying makes sense

Buy when the complete payment, including tax, insurance, and upkeep, fits without strain. Buy when you expect to stay at least five years. Buy when you still have a reserve after the down payment and closing costs. And watch for areas where rent sits close to the owning payment, which happens on the east side and in Redmond before it does on the west side of Bend.
