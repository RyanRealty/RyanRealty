Why this question comes up so often

Most people who plan a move to Central Oregon start by searching Bend. Many of them end up buying in Redmond or Sisters instead, and most are happy with how it turned out. The three towns are close together and look out on the same mountains, but daily life in each is quite different. Here is how we help buyers think it through.

The real decision

Forget the town names for a moment. You are weighing three things. First, how much house your budget buys. Second, how near you want to be to the outdoors that drew you here. Third, how much town you want around you. Each place answers those questions differently.

Bend

Bend is the region's hub. It has the hospital, a university branch, the breweries, the Old Mill District and a trail network that follows the river. The ski hill at Mt. Bachelor is about 21 miles from downtown by road, closer from here than from the other two towns.

Homes range from older cottages on the west side to newer subdivisions on the east and southeast, and the price difference between those areas is significant. Choose Bend if you want to walk or bike to errands, if your job is here, or if you want the biggest selection. Expect to pay the most for a given amount of space.

Redmond

Redmond sits about 17 miles north of downtown Bend along the main highway. It has the regional airport, the fairgrounds, a downtown that has filled in nicely over the past decade, and most of the area's newer starter neighborhoods.

Your budget goes further here, both for the house and the lot, and the drive into Bend takes roughly 25 minutes when traffic is light. Smith Rock and the Crooked River are also nearer. Choose Redmond if you want a newer place, a proper yard and a commute you can live with.

Sisters

Sisters is about 22 miles northwest of downtown Bend, right at the foot of the Three Sisters peaks. It is a small town with a western feel, a rodeo, a folk festival and a quilt show that packs the streets every July.

Homes include in-town lots, acreage on the outskirts, and the Black Butte Ranch area a few miles west. Because the town is small, there is not much to choose from at any moment, so buyers often wait for the right house rather than comparing many. Choose Sisters for quiet, a walkable center and mountain access, as long as you do not need to be in Bend every day.

A quick way to sort it

Most choice and activity: Bend.

Most house for the money with a short drive: Redmond.

Smallest, quietest and nearest the peaks: Sisters.

For flying out, Redmond wins. For hospital care, Bend does. For the ski hill, Bend is closest.

Common questions

Is Redmond cheaper than Bend? Per square foot, Redmond has regularly closed below Bend. Current medians and the gap between them are on the live market pages.

Is Sisters a cheaper option? Not reliably. With a small housing stock and many acreage and custom homes, its median can land near or above Bend's in a given month. People choose it for the setting, not a discount.

How long is the drive between towns? Bend to Redmond is about 17 miles, around 25 minutes. Bend to Sisters is about 22 miles, around 35 minutes. Summer weekends and snow days stretch both.

What about remote work? All three towns have fiber or cable in town, but rural parcels need to be checked address by address. We confirm the provider for a specific property before you make an offer.

Should you rent first? If you have never seen a winter here, a few months of renting is a fair way to test the drive, the snow and the town.
