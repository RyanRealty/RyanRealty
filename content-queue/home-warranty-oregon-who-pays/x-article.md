A question we hear from people relocating to Central Oregon: who pays for the home warranty? The short version is that it depends entirely on what the buyer and seller put in writing.

Is a home warranty required?

No. Neither Oregon's license law nor the rules of the state real estate agency demand a warranty plan on a residential sale. A plan exists only if the two parties decide they want one.

Where the decision lives

The standard Oregon residential sale agreement includes a section just for this. It works like a short checklist. You indicate whether a plan will be purchased for the buyer. You write in which plan it is and what it costs. Then you indicate whether the buyer or the seller is the one who orders it and pays for it.

The printed form is neutral. It doesn't nudge toward the buyer and it doesn't nudge toward the seller. That means there is no built-in default to fall back on, and the signed version of the section is the only thing that counts.

Don't import expectations from another state

In some places, buyers expect the seller to include a plan as a courtesy, or sellers expect the buyer to handle it. Oregon doesn't work from that kind of habit. If you want the seller to cover the cost, ask for that in the offer itself, or put it into a signed addendum. If you are the seller and would rather not pay, the same logic applies in the other direction.

Once the contract says the buyer pays, that is the deal. Changing it later takes a written change that both sides sign. A verbal understanding, or an assumption about how things are done back home, won't override the paper.

A different kind of warranty on the disclosure form

Sellers sometimes confuse two separate things. The seller's property disclosure form does include a question about warranties, but it is aimed at specific building materials and products, such as something installed in the structure that carries a manufacturer's coverage. It has nothing to do with a home warranty plan that a buyer or seller purchases at closing. Answering one doesn't answer the other.

What does a plan cover?

That varies from plan to plan. Coverage options, exclusions and limits come from the warranty company, so the most reliable move is to read the plan terms before you agree to include one. Your broker can explain the choices and help you compare, and the company behind the plan can tell you what it does and doesn't cover.

A practical way to handle it

If you are a buyer, decide before you write the offer whether a plan matters to you, and if so, who you want paying. Put it in the first draft rather than raising it late in negotiation.

If you are a seller, think about whether offering a plan could make your home easier to say yes to, and if you would, decide whether you'd cover it or ask the buyer to. Either answer is fine. The point is to make it a deliberate choice and write it down clearly.

If you are on either side and something in the section is unclear, ask before signing. A few minutes spent on one checkbox is far easier than sorting out a disagreement at closing.
