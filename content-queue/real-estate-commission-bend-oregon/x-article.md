Commission used to feel like one fixed number. Today it is two separate conversations, and Oregon has added paperwork rules around both. Here is a plain walkthrough for anyone planning to sell in Bend.

Why this feels confusing

People hear the word commission and picture a single percentage that every agent charges. In reality, no law sets what a brokerage charges to list your home, and no law sets what a buyer's agent receives. Both are negotiated. What changed in recent years is how and when those conversations happen, and what has to be written down.

Our listing fee in dollars

We charge 3% of what your home sells for, and we do not add extra fees on top. Take a $765,000 sale, which is the median sale price in Bend over the past year as of October 8, 2026. At that price our fee is $22,950. You do not pay it up front. It comes out of your proceeds at closing.

For that fee, you get the MLS listing, professional photography, video, a 3D tour, the full marketing plan, every showing, weekly written reports, and transaction management until the deal closes. The fee is stated in your listing agreement, and Oregon requires that agreement to be on paper before a broker begins marketing your home. It also cannot run longer than 24 months.

Who pays the buyer's agent

For years, the listing side typically posted an offer of compensation to the buyer's agent right in the MLS. Under the practice changes that took effect on August 17, 2024, that posting is no longer allowed. Buyers who work with an agent now sign a written agreement before touring, and that agreement states what their agent will be paid.

As a seller, you can still choose to cover some, all, or none of that fee. It simply becomes a term of each offer. Some buyers will ask for help with it and some will not. You can accept, decline, or counter, the same way you would with price.

Here is the arithmetic. On a $765,000 sale, every half-point is $3,825. That is a number, not a recommendation. We build a seller net sheet that shows what you keep from each offer after any buyer's agent fee, so you can answer with the full picture in front of you.

What Oregon requires on paper

Since January 1, 2025, ORS 696.810 has required a broker who represents a buyer of a home, residential land, or a one-to-four-unit property to work under a written representation agreement. It has to be signed before, or as soon as reasonably practicable after, the broker starts helping the buyer. It cannot last longer than 24 months including renewals, and it must explain how the buyer's agent may be paid, along with the term, how to end it, and whether it is exclusive. MLS rules are stricter on timing and want it signed before the first tour.

What else comes out of your proceeds

Commission is the largest line, but not the only one. On that same $765,000 sale, the owner's title policy, your half of escrow, the lien search, and recording the release of your mortgage come to about $3,215, or 0.42% of the price. Deschutes County has no transfer tax. Your loan payoff and the property tax proration also come from proceeds.

How to compare agents on fees

You will see commission rates quoted online for Bend. We have not found a published, dated measure of what local sellers actually paid, so we do not quote one. Instead, ask each agent you interview for three answers in writing. First, the fee as a percent and in dollars at your likely price. Second, what that fee includes. Third, how they would respond if a buyer asks you to cover their agent's compensation.

The short version

Two fees, both negotiable, neither set by law. Get every term on paper, and ask for the dollar figures before you sign anything.
