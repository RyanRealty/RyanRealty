Selling a house in Bend comes down to a handful of decisions made in the right order. The ones you make before the sign goes up shape nearly everything that follows. Here is the sequence we follow with every owner.

Start with an honest price

A good price is built from three groups of homes: those that sold nearby recently, those currently under contract, and those competing for your buyers right now. We adjust for size, condition, lot, and view, then hand you a written valuation with the comps attached. It is free, and you do not have to sign a listing agreement to get it.

If the figure comes in lower than you hoped, better to learn that immediately than after weeks of silence on the market.

Then run your own math. Your proceeds are the sale price minus the listing fee, any buyer-agent compensation you agree to, title and escrow, prorated taxes, your loan payoff, and whatever you spent getting the home ready.

Prep that actually matters

Buyers in Central Oregon tour several homes in a day and compare them in their heads. What sticks is what they see right away: a roof with life left, working systems, kitchen and baths that feel clean and reasonably current, floors and paint that do not look like a project.

Improvements that only change how the listing photos look can usually be skipped. We walk through the house with you, estimate what each item costs, and tell you whether it affects price. You make the call.

Oregon also requires a seller property disclosure on most residential sales. Fill it out fully. A careful disclosure protects you after the sale is done.

The launch

We sign, schedule photography and the sign, and begin marketing. The home appears in the MLS as coming soon, then goes live once everything is ready. Professional photography, a tour, and the marketing plan are all covered by our 3% plan, with no add-on fees.

The opening stretch matters most. Buyers with saved searches see the home the day it launches, and agents with matching clients send it along that evening. The first weekend of showings is the biggest crowd the home will see. A correct price meets that crowd. A wrong one wastes it.

Showings and weekly reports

Every showing is confirmed with you through an appointment system. Each week you get a written report covering how many people came through, what they said, and what has sold or gone under contract nearby since the listing began. If people are visiting but no offers arrive, the feedback shows whether the issue is condition or price. We change one thing at a time.

Reading an offer

An offer is far more than a price. It includes financing, earnest money, inspection and appraisal contingencies, a closing date, and often requests for help with the buyer's agent fee or closing costs. Seller credits have become common here.

So we send every offer with a net sheet. You weigh the amount you would receive at closing instead of the number on top. We counter on the terms you care about, and when several offers arrive we line them up side by side.

Inspection, appraisal, and closing

After acceptance, the buyer usually inspects within the first two weeks and may ask for repairs or a credit. We tell you what is reasonable and what is not. If the buyer has a loan, the lender orders an appraisal, and a price rooted in real comps tends to hold.

Title and escrow prepare the paperwork. You sign electronically or with a notary. Taxes and HOA dues are prorated to closing day, your loan is paid off, and the remainder is wired to you. A typical financed sale in Oregon wraps up within about six weeks of acceptance.

Living somewhere else?

Every step still works from a distance. We hold the keys, manage vendors, and handle mail, snow, and security checks while the home is listed, and you sign from wherever you are.
