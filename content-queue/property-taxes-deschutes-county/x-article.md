Oregon handles property tax differently from most states, and the difference matters whether you are buying or selling in Bend. Here is a plain walkthrough, drawn from the Oregon Department of Revenue and Deschutes County.

The core idea

Most people assume the county taxes what a home is worth today. Oregon does not. Voters passed Measure 50 in 1997, and it gave every property a maximum assessed value that can grow only 3% a year, whatever the market does. The tax is figured on whichever is lower, that capped figure or the real market value.

Central Oregon prices have climbed much faster than that cap for most of the years since. So for most homes, the number the county taxes sits well under what the house would sell for. A home owned for decades has decades of limited growth built in. A brand new home enters the rolls nearer to market. That is how two houses on the same street end up with very different bills.

What a sale does and does not change

This is the point that surprises people moving from places like California. Buying a home here does not reset the assessed value to your price. It keeps following its own capped path. The bill you see before you buy is a good guide to the bill you will get after.

What owners pay on average

For fiscal 2025-26, the state reports an average consolidated rate for the county of $16.80 per $1,000 of assessed value. Measured against real market value, the effective rate comes to about 0.698%. Applied to the recent ninety-day median sale price in Bend of $733,000, that is roughly $5,116 a year. Treat that as a ballpark only. Your own rate depends on your tax code area, the particular mix of city, school, fire, and bond levies where the home sits.

Look up the actual account

The county runs a public lookup called DIAL, at dial.deschutes.org. Search by address, owner, or account number. You will see the assessed value, the market value, the tax code area, and the current bill. Buyers should check the house before making an offer. Sellers should check their own home and the comparable sales.

The payment calendar

Statements go out by October 25. Pay everything by November 15 and you receive a 3% discount. Pay two thirds by then and the discount is 2%. Otherwise the bill is split into three payments, due November 15, February 15, and May 15. When a deadline lands on a weekend it moves to the next business day, which makes the 2026 date Monday, November 16. The tax year itself runs from July to June.

Budgeting as a buyer

Use the current DIAL bill for your payment estimate. Your lender collects a prorated share at closing, then a monthly amount through escrow. Insurance is the other big piece of that monthly payment, and here it varies more from address to address than tax does.

What happens at closing

Escrow divides the tax year to the day. If the seller already paid the full year, the buyer's portion is credited back on the settlement statement. If it is unpaid, the seller's portion through closing comes out of their proceeds. Either way, we build the tax picture into the net sheet that goes out with every offer.

Relief programs for 2026

Oregon does not freeze taxes for older owners. It offers a deferral instead: the state pays the tax, records a lien, and collects the balance with 6% annual interest when the home sells or transfers. Eligibility includes age 62 or older, or being disabled and qualifying for Social Security disability, with household income under $70,000 and at least five full years of owning and living in the home.

Disabled veterans and surviving spouses can exempt $27,092 or $32,512 of assessed value, depending on category, with claims filed by April 1. Active-duty service members have a separate exemption set by statute, and the county assessor quotes the current figure when you file.

If the value looks wrong

You can appeal to the county's Property Value Appeals Board. The petition is due by December 31, or the next business day. The argument is about real market value on the assessment date, so recent comparable sales carry the most weight.
