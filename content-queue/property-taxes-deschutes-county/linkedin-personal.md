Out-of-state buyers ask me the same thing in nearly every first conversation: will my property tax jump to match my purchase price?

In Oregon, the answer is no, and the reason is worth knowing. Measure 50 in 1997 fixed a maximum assessed value for every property and limited its yearly growth to 3%. Tax is charged on that figure, which in Central Oregon usually trails market value by a wide margin. A home held for decades carries decades of capped growth. A newer build enters the rolls closer to market. Two similar houses on one street can have very different bills.

The practical move is simple. Every Deschutes County account is public on the DIAL lookup. Before you make an offer, pull up the house and read the assessed value, the market value, the tax code area, and the current bill. Before you list, pull up your own home and the comps.

A few other things worth knowing: statements go out by October 25, paying in full by November 15 earns a 3% discount, and escrow splits the tax year to the day at closing. There are also deferral and exemption programs for seniors, disabled owners, and veterans. I wrote it all up in one place, with the sources.
