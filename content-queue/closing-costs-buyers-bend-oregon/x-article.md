The price on the offer is only part of what a Bend buyer brings to the table. Down payment, closing costs and prepaid items all show up together, and most of them can be estimated well ahead of time. Oregon regulates some of these charges, and federal rules limit how much the others can shift. Here is how the pieces fit.

Three buckets of cash

The down payment covers the portion of the price that no lender is financing. Closing costs are what it takes to set up the loan and complete the sale. Prepaids are money collected up front: a year of homeowners insurance, interest from closing day through month end, and a portion of property taxes. Add the three and you have your cash to close. Your lender must hand you the Closing Disclosure no later than three business days ahead of signing, and that document shows the total.

What the lender charges

Origination, underwriting and appraisal charges come from the lender, and they differ from one lender to the next. That is the reason to compare Loan Estimates rather than just rates. Under federal TRID rules, fees the lender itself sets are held to zero tolerance, so they cannot rise after the Loan Estimate. Certain outside charges can climb, but only up to 10% in total. For a sense of scale, a VA fee schedule that took effect May 1, 2026 lists $850 for a single-family appraisal in Oregon, and conventional appraisals in Central Oregon land in a similar range.

Title, escrow and recording

A title policy for the lender protects the loan, and buyers normally pay for it. Around Central Oregon, sellers usually cover the owner's policy and the two sides split the escrow fee. Those are customs, not laws, and an offer can divide them differently. The Deschutes County clerk charges $102 to record page one of a deed or mortgage, plus $5 for every extra page, per the clerk's rates starting July 1, 2026.

Prepaids and the tax year

Property taxes in Oregon follow a July 1 to June 30 year. At closing, escrow divides the current year's taxes up to the day the sale closes, so you pay your share and the seller pays theirs.

No transfer tax

Oregon has no state tax on real estate transfers, and Deschutes County adds none. Washington County, near Portland, is the only Oregon county that does.

FHA and VA fees

FHA loans carry an upfront mortgage insurance premium of 1.75% of the base loan amount, and you can roll it into the loan. The annual premium on a 30-year loan with under 5% down, at or below the FHA base limit, is 0.55% of the loan per year, paid monthly.

VA purchase loans carry a funding fee. It is 2.15% for a first-time user putting less than 5% down. A repeat user pays 3.3%. Putting 5% or more down drops it to 1.5%, and 10% or more brings it to 1.25%. Veterans collecting disability compensation from the VA for a service-connected condition do not pay it. Plenty of veterans here are exempt and never realize it, so have your lender look.

Seller credits and buydowns

A seller credit toward your costs reduces the cash you bring while the price stays put. A seller-funded rate buydown lowers your monthly payment instead. Both have turned up more often in Bend as inventory has grown, and both are requested in the offer. Your loan program limits the size of a credit, so find out the cap before drafting the request.

Reading the paperwork

Your Loan Estimate arrives in three business days after you apply. Lay it beside the Closing Disclosure when that arrives. Should any lender charge have shifted, ask why before you put pen to paper.

Cash buyers

Paying cash trims the list but does not erase it. Nothing goes to origination or a loan title policy, yet the escrow fee, recording fee, prorated taxes and, if chosen, a first year of insurance remain.
