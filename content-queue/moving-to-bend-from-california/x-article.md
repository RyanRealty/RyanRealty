Californians make up a large part of the relocation business we see at Ryan Realty LLC. They usually arrive with proceeds from a home sale, work that lets them live almost anywhere, and questions that begin with dollars and end with snowfall. Here is how we walk them through it.

Why people look at Bend

The pull is consistent. A smaller city with a lively downtown. Mountains and a river within city limits. Four seasons. And a housing dollar that goes further than it did in the Bay Area, Los Angeles, or San Diego. Bend is not a bargain town, and we say so plainly. But the same budget generally buys more house and more land than it does in the California metros people are leaving.

The tax trade

Purchases here carry no sales tax, and nearly everyone notices it first, from a vehicle to furniture. The catch is that Oregon taxes income, and the top rate will look familiar to many California earners. So this is a trade, not a gift. The current brackets are published by the state revenue department. Before you decide, ask your accountant to model both states using your real income.

Closing costs and property tax

Outside one county near Portland, Oregon has no state transfer tax on real estate. In Deschutes County, that line on your closing statement is zero.

Property tax also works on a different logic than in California. Bills are based on an assessed value, and the state limits how much that figure can rise each year. The result: a home held for many years can carry a much smaller bill than a similar house nearby that just changed hands. Do not assume the sale price is the number your tax will follow.

Insurance is the surprise

Much of Central Oregon sits in an area exposed to wildfire, and homeowners insurance pricing varies widely from one address to the next. Request a quote for the particular property before you submit an offer, not afterward. It can change what a house really costs you each month.

Visit when it is not peak summer

Everyone tours in summer. That is when Bend is busiest, dustiest, and most affected by wildfire smoke. Plan a trip in the cold months. Drive on snowy roads. Notice how the west side and east side of town clear differently. Find out whether you enjoy real winter or just the idea of it. If only a single trip is possible, choose spring or fall, and ask us how the other seasons treat the home you like.

Bend, Redmond, or Sisters

Plenty of California buyers begin in Bend and land somewhere else. Redmond often offers a newer home on a larger lot, with the airport a short drive away. Sisters is smaller and quieter, with the mountains close by. Inside Bend itself, the west side and east side differ in price and feel, so that split deserves its own look.

Buying before you move

Most of our California clients purchase before they relocate. We show homes by video, forward disclosures and inspection reports the same day they reach us, and work with your lender and a title company for a remote closing with electronic signatures and a nearby notary.

If your California home has not sold yet, you face a timing choice. You can make an offer contingent on that sale, or close in California first and rent in Bend for a while. A contingent offer is a harder sell when the seller has competing interest, and perfectly reasonable when a home has lingered. We read the individual listing and tell you which situation you are in.

Quick answers

Is Bend cheaper than the Bay Area or Southern California? For most buyers we work with, on housing, yes. Compare Bend's current median with the median where you live now, and keep in mind that Bend is priced like a resort town by Oregon standards.

What surprises people most? Winter driving, late summer smoke, insurance that varies by address, and the gap between west side and east side prices. The missing sales tax is the happy surprise.
