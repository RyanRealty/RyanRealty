Bend can look closed to someone who has never bought a home. It is not. It does ask for three things: a plan, an honest budget, and a willingness to look beyond the west side. Here is how we walk first-time buyers through it in Bend, Redmond, and the towns nearby.

Start with the lender, not the listings

Before you tour anything, get a complete pre-approval. That is different from an online prequalification. A pre-approval means a lender has actually reviewed your income, credit, and savings, and can tell you what they will lend and what the monthly payment looks like.

Ask to see that payment with property taxes and homeowners insurance built in for a home at your target price. Insurance here can differ from one address to the next because of wildfire exposure, and that single line can change what you are able to afford. Sellers in Bend rarely take an offer seriously without a pre-approval behind it, and the payment is what sets your search, so it needs to come first.

Oregon also has programs aimed at first-time buyers, including a bond loan program run through the state housing agency and a savings account that carries a state income tax benefit. Terms and income limits change, so check the agencies directly and ask your lender whether you qualify.

What your budget tends to buy

Price bands move every month, so we do not print them. The pattern is steadier. At the entry level in Bend, the options are condos and townhomes, older homes on the east side, and smaller homes in neighborhoods farther from downtown. The same money in Redmond usually buys a newer single-family home with a yard. Go farther out to La Pine or Prineville and you get more house again, in exchange for a longer drive.

The current median figures live on the Bend and Redmond market pages, and a separate comparison covers the tradeoffs between Bend, Redmond, and Sisters.

How the purchase unfolds

The sequence is simple. Pre-approval comes first. Then you search, with alerts set for your preferred districts and price band, and you tour in batches so the homes are easy to compare. When one feels right, we review recent closed sales and write the offer that same day, with earnest money in line with what comparable homes have closed with.

Once a seller accepts, the inspection period begins, followed by the appraisal. Then the loan is finalized and closing documents are signed. A financed purchase in Oregon typically closes in a month to six weeks after acceptance.

Inspections that fit this region

Every buyer should have a general inspection. Central Oregon adds a few more possibilities. A home outside city sewer has a septic system, and a home outside city water has a well. Each needs its own inspection, and a well also needs a water test. Older west side homes deserve a close look at the roof, the sewer line, and the electrical panel. Homes near wildland areas may come with defensible-space requirements that influence insurance.

If an inspection reveals a problem, you have choices. You can ask for repairs, ask for a credit, or walk away within the inspection period and recover your earnest money.

Your agent agreement

Since August 2024, buyers sign a written agreement with their agent before touring. It spells out what the agent does and how they are paid, and the offer asks the seller to cover that fee. You see the figure before the first showing, so nothing is a surprise.

Mistakes worth skipping

Touring before financing is in place, then losing a home to a buyer who had it.

Browsing only the west side of Bend and deciding nothing is within reach.

Skipping the well or septic inspection on a rural property.

Giving up the inspection to win a bidding war.

Underestimating cash to close, which is your down payment plus closing costs plus prepaid taxes and insurance.

Taking a new job or opening new credit between pre-approval and closing.

After closing

Your first property tax statement will reflect assessed value, which in Oregon rises on a capped schedule instead of jumping to what you paid. Keep your inspection report, disclosures, and closing statement together in one place. You will want them again when you sell.
