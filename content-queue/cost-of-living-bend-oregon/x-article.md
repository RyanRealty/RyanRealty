Bend is a beautiful place to live, and it is priced like one. Before you start touring, it helps to know which costs are large, which are small, and which depend on the exact address. Here is a plain walk through the budget, using figures pulled on September 7, 2026.

Housing sets the tone

Look at recent closings through September 7, 2026. Bend's typical single-family price was $733,000. Redmond's was $499,000. Those two numbers explain most of the difference between the towns, because taxes, fuel, and utilities come from the same state rules and regional providers.

Turning price into a payment

At a national average rate of 6.71% and 20% down, the Bend figure comes to roughly $4,214 a month once property tax is added, and that is before insurance. The Redmond version is about $2,869. Rates change weekly and prices change daily, so think of these as a September 2026 snapshot, then ask a lender to run the real address.

West side, east side, and nearby towns

Inside Bend, the west side generally costs more per square foot than the east side for a comparable home. Outside Bend, the same money buys more house in Redmond, and more again in La Pine and Prineville, in exchange for a longer drive.

The Oregon tax trade

There is no general sales tax here. The trade is a state income tax with brackets from 4.75% up to 9.9%. Whether that works in your favor depends on your income and your current state, so run your own numbers through both.

Property tax is charged on assessed value rather than market value, and Measure 50 limits how fast that assessed value can grow, to 3% a year. That means two similar houses next door to each other can have very different bills if one changed hands recently. The county's average effective rate is about 0.7% of real market value, and the bill for any address is public on the county lookup.

Insurance, utilities, and getting around

Homeowners insurance varies the most by address. Much of Central Oregon is exposed to wildfire, so two homes a mile apart can be quoted very differently. Get a quote on the specific house before you write an offer.

For utilities, ask the seller for a year of electric and gas bills during the inspection period. City water and sewer follow a published rate schedule.

Fuel costs more here than in most of the country. On September 7, 2026, Oregon's average was $5.02 a gallon against $4.15 nationally. The good news is that Bend drives short, and most crosstown trips take minutes outside the summer peak.

Why people pay it

The mountain, the river, and the trails are the reason most people move here. A full adult season pass at the local ski area was $1,399 on September 7, 2026. The river trail runs right through the city, and much of the other outdoor life costs only gas and gear.

Set all of this next to the income side. The median household income is $96,394 according to Census data. Next to the payment on a median house, that gap is the town's central tension, and it is why Redmond keeps absorbing demand that starts in Bend.

A budget in five steps

1. Pick a price from the live market page for the town and side of town you want.
2. Have a lender turn it into a payment with taxes and insurance for a real address.
3. Run your income through Oregon's brackets and compare with where you live now.
4. Add the recurring costs that are yours: the pass, a second car, the commute.
5. Tour with that number, not with the list prices.
