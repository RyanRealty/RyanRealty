Sellers usually ask about marketing first. We think cost deserves the first question, because nearly all of it can be known before you ever list.

The short version

Take a $765,000 home in Bend, the median sale price in our MLS data as of Oct 8, 2026. If you do not cover the buyer's agent, the sale costs $26,165, or 3.4% of the price. If you agree to cover an illustrative 2.5%, it costs $45,290, or 5.9%. Both totals leave out your loan payoff and prorated property taxes, which come out of your proceeds separately.

What changed about agent pay

Not long ago, the listing fee and the buyer's agent pay were a single figure. Since August 2024, they are separate. Offers of compensation no longer appear in the MLS, and a buyer signs a written agreement with their own agent that states the fee. Whether you help with that fee is a term of each offer, and you decide it one offer at a time. That is why the second column of our example is labeled illustrative. The 2.5% is an example, not a standard rate.

We walk sellers through this trade before the first offer arrives, so nobody is making the decision under pressure.

What our listing fee covers

Our fee is 3% of the sale price, with no add-on fees. It includes the MLS listing, professional photography, a 3D tour, the marketing plan, every showing, and transaction management through close. Commission is negotiable, and every listing agreement is its own conversation.

Title, escrow, and recording

These are the steadier lines. On the example sale, the owner's title policy is $1,748. Title premiums in Oregon are filed rates, so every title company charges the same amount, and in Central Oregon the seller customarily pays it. Your half of escrow is about $1,340. The lien search is $25. Recording the release of your mortgage is $102. Together, those items come to about $3,215.

Transfer tax

In Deschutes County, this line is zero. Oregon law bars new local transfer taxes, and Washington County holds the only one in the state, which was already in place before the ban.

Prorations, payoffs, and HOAs

Escrow divides the year's property tax at your closing date, so you cover only the period you held title. If the bill is still unpaid, your share comes out of proceeds. If you already paid it, the buyer's share comes back to you. Your mortgage is paid off from the sale money as well.

If the home sits in a homeowners association, that association sets its own transfer charges, and some Bend communities base them on a percentage of the sale price. Ask early so it does not surprise you.

Oregon withholding applies only to nonresident sellers. It is a tax prepayment rather than a fee, and Oregon residents are exempt.

Work on the house

The listing fee does not pay for repairs, cleaning, or staging. Those are yours to decide. We provide a list of what each item is likely to cost and whether it would change your price, then you choose what is worth doing.

See your net before you say yes

Ahead of accepting any offer, you receive a net sheet from us. It shows the price, the listing fee, any buyer-agent pay you agreed to, title and escrow, prorations, your payoff, and any credit to the buyer, with your net at the bottom. That is the number to plan around. When the final settlement statement arrives from escrow, it should match the net sheet within the prorations, and we go through it with you line by line.
