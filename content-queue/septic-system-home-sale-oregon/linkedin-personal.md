Septic systems are one of those details that can quietly trip up a home sale.

In Oregon, the standard contract does not decide who pumps the tank or who pays for an inspection. That gets settled in a septic addendum the buyer attaches (OREF 081). Each service gets its own checkbox, and each one gets its own payer. Nothing is pre-filled for you.

A few things I tell clients. The seller hands over whatever records exist, like past inspections, maintenance contracts, permits and as-built drawings. Inspections have to come from a DEQ-certified professional. And if the reports show substantial deficiencies, the buyer sends a list, both sides get a short window to negotiate, and the buyer can walk with deposits refunded if no deal is reached. Staying silent past that window means accepting the system as is.

On the maintenance side, DEQ suggests an inspection every 5 to 7 years and pumping once solids pass 40 percent.
