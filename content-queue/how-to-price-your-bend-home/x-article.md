Your list price is the one choice a seller makes that the market answers almost immediately. Set it where buyers are already choosing and the tours follow. Set it where you wish it would land and the house sits, then sells for less after a reduction. Here is how we approach pricing in Bend, and what we do when a listing stalls.

Three groups of homes set the number

Closed sales show what buyers have paid for similar homes in recent months. Pending sales show what buyers are agreeing to right now, before the closing appears in any report. Active listings are your competition, the homes someone will walk through on the same weekend as yours.

Ignore any one group and the price becomes a guess. We pull all three for your district and price band, then account for square footage, condition, lot, and views. What comes out is a range you can explain to an appraiser later.

Citywide medians are useful background, but they are not your number. Yours comes from the few closings nearest your home in both location and timing.

The early window is expensive to miss

A fresh listing gets its largest share of attention at the start. Buyers with saved searches see it the day it appears. Agents forward it to clients that evening. The opening weekend of tours is the biggest the home will see.

A correct price meets that crowd. A price that is too high lets it walk past. Shoppers who skipped it do not return by themselves when the price falls, and by then the listing shows a days-on-market figure that looks like a red flag.

Condition and price move together

Buyers measure a home against the others they saw that day, not against what it could become. A house with an original kitchen and a newer roof will not price like a fully renovated one a couple of streets away, even at the same size.

So a seller has two levers: what to repair before listing, and where to price whatever remains. Some fixes change the price. Others only change how the photos look. Do the first kind and skip the second.

Read the feedback like data

Tours without offers are not a failure. They are information.

If buyers are coming through and the comments focus on condition, the price is probably close and the presentation needs work. If comments focus on cost, or tours fade after the opening weekend, the price sits above where comparable homes are closing. If nobody comes at all, either the price is far off or the photos are not doing their job.

We send sellers a written report each week with showing counts, comments, and what has sold or gone under contract nearby.

When to cut

A reduction is not an admission of defeat. It is effectively a second launch, and it works when it is bold. A tiny trim that leaves the home above the nearest closed sale accomplishes nothing. A cut that lands at or slightly below what buyers have been paying revives the alerts and draws back people who passed.

A single well-sized move beats a string of small ones, because each small step adds days without adding tours.

Credits change your net

In today's Bend market, many closed sales include a seller credit for the buyer's closing costs or a rate buydown. A credit reduces what you keep just as a lower price does, and buyers ask for them.

That is why we model your proceeds three ways: at the list price, at the likely offer, and with a credit included. You should plan around what lands in your account, not what is printed on the sign.

A simple next step

A written valuation starts with your address and includes the comps behind it. You do not need to sign a listing agreement to get the number.
